    Compass

    Alignment nudge: 0°

    Phone compasses drift; if the sky sits a few degrees off, drag it sideways while pointing to nudge the alignment, or centre a bright star you can see. On an iPhone the compass only reads true while the phone is tilted toward flat, so tilt it down for a moment now and then and the heading re-calibrates; upright, it holds the last good fix.

    The brightest satellites (CelesTrak's "visual" list) plus the space stations, propagated with SGP4 from the latest two-line elements. Bright markers are sunlit satellites in a dark sky, the ones you can actually see; dim ones are in the Earth's shadow or up in daylight.

    About

    Stars to magnitude 6 with their colours, constellation figures and the Messier objects from d3-celestial (Hipparcos data, BSD-3); the Sun, Moon and planets from low-precision elements (about an arcminute); satellites from CelesTrak; magnetic declination from NOAA's WMM2025. Positions include precession to today's equinox but not refraction, so objects right on the horizon sit a little higher than drawn.
