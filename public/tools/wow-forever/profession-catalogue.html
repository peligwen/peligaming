    The route

    Set your skill and where you are going. At every skill on the way the route makes whatever gives the next point for the least in materials (or, if you ask for the quickest, in the least time at the anvil, counting what has to be made on the side: this build's crafts run from three seconds for a sharpening stone to a minute for a breastplate), out of the recipes you have by then. It counts what you have already made as yours to use, so the grinding stones from one step feed the bracers of the next, and anything one of your recipes can make is made rather than bought. It stays on a recipe while that is within a twentieth of the best, and leaves one whose chance has dropped under the mark you set if something better is to hand.

    What you make to use is made early. A bolt of cloth takes the same cloth whenever it is made, but made while bolts are still orange it brings a point with it, and made when a later step calls for it, long after bolts have gone grey, it brings nothing. So where a step needs something one of your recipes makes, the route makes it as soon as that recipe can be cast (or on the front of the step where the route takes that recipe up anyway) and for as long as a craft can still give a point, and marks the step made ahead. It makes ahead only what the route goes on to use, and keeps the result only if the whole route comes out cheaper (or quicker) for it; the line above the steps says by how much.

    Tools are part of it. Where a recipe is made with a tool, the first step that needs one gets it: an enchanter's rod is made, a blacksmith's hammer bought, and its materials are on the shopping list. Made while its own recipe is still orange, a rod is a step of its own, one craft and a point with it; later than that it is made on the side before the step that needs it. A better tool does the work of a lesser one, as in the game (a Runed Golden Rod will do wherever a copper or a silver one is asked for), so only the rods the route cannot do without are made. The ticks under the sum say which tools you already have. To start with you are taken to have the ones you could have made or bought before your skill; untick any you lack.

    What a trainer charges is counted too, for the recipes the route uses and for no others, and for each rank on the way. At the trainer lists them by the skill each is wanted at, with the kind of trainer that teaches it: a Journeyman Tailor's list stops long before an Artisan's. If you want the cheapest route, a recipe is only learned, and a tool only made, where the route comes to less for it: one taken up for a point or two that saves less than its fee is left out, and the line above the steps says which. Say know it or have it for what you have already paid for, and it costs nothing. All of this is vanilla's: fees, levels and who teaches what are the server's business and no client carries them, so they are marked † and Forever's may differ.

    “My recipes” are the ones a trainer teaches and the ones you start with; tick a plan, pattern or formula in the list once you own it and the route will use it, or untick anything you cannot get. “Every recipe” shows the best route the game allows and the plans it would take. Recipes on a cooldown are left out unless you tick them. Where nothing you have gives a point, the route says so and names the cheapest thing that would.

    Crafts are given twice: what it takes on average, and what is enough nine times out of ten, worked out exactly from the chance at each point. The shopping list can be switched to the second; it then buys for every step as if that step went badly, which is more than a whole run of bad luck would need.

    Reading the table

    The four numbers are the skill at which a recipe turns orange, yellow, green and grey. Yellow and grey are the client's own numbers for the recipe; green is the point halfway between them; orange starts where the recipe can be learned, and whatever your skill, a recipe below its yellow level is orange. The bar draws the same four on a scale of 1 to 300, with a white line at your skill.

    Where a recipe is learned from an item, the first number is the skill that item asks for, and it is exact. What a trainer asks is decided on the server and no client carries it, so those levels are marked: † is what vanilla's trainers asked, for a recipe Forever has left where it was; ≈ is vanilla's level moved by as much as Forever moved the recipe's yellow level; ? is a recipe no item teaches and no vanilla trainer taught, so its level is not in the data. Open a recipe a trainer teaches and it says which of vanilla's trainers taught it and for how much; searching for a trainer's title (“artisan tailor”) lists what that trainer is the first to teach.

    How the chance is estimated

    Two things are certain: an orange recipe gives a point every time, and a grey one never does. Blizzard has not published what happens in between. This page uses the straight line players have long used for Classic: certain up to the yellow level, then falling evenly to nothing at grey,

    chance = (grey − skill) ÷ (grey − yellow)

    which is 100% on the first yellow point, 50% as the recipe turns green and 0% as it turns grey, and averages 75% across the yellow band and 25% across the green one. Forever may not use exactly this curve, so read every percentage here as an estimate, good for comparing recipes and planning materials rather than for counting on the last craft.

    Working Overtime

    The tooltip does not say how that bonus is applied, and the arithmetic is on the server. So the page offers both readings: × multiplies the chance (at five ranks, 50% becomes 60%), + adds to it (50% becomes 70%). Either way an orange recipe stays certain and a grey one still gives nothing. × is the default because it is the lower of the two estimates.
