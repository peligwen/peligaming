    The route

    Set your skill and where you are going. At every skill on the way the route makes whatever gives the next point for the least in materials (or, if you ask for the quickest, in the least time at the anvil: this build's crafts run from three seconds for a sharpening stone to a minute for a breastplate), out of the recipes you have by then. It counts what you have already made as yours to use, so the grinding stones from one step feed the bracers of the next, and anything one of your recipes can make is made rather than bought. It stays on a recipe while that is within a twentieth of the best, and leaves one whose chance has dropped under the mark you set if something better is to hand.

    “My recipes” are the ones a trainer teaches and the ones you start with; tick a plan, pattern or formula in the list once you own it and the route will use it, or untick anything you cannot get. “Every recipe” shows the best route the game allows and the plans it would take. Recipes on a cooldown are left out unless you tick them. Where nothing you have gives a point, the route says so and names the cheapest thing that would.

    Crafts are given twice: what it takes on average, and what is enough nine times out of ten, worked out exactly from the chance at each point. The shopping list can be switched to the second; it then buys for every step as if that step went badly, which is more than a whole run of bad luck would need.

    Reading the table

    The four numbers are the skill at which a recipe turns orange, yellow, green and grey. Yellow and grey are the client's own numbers for the recipe; green is the point halfway between them; orange starts where the recipe can be learned, and whatever your skill, a recipe below its yellow level is orange. The bar draws the same four on a scale of 1 to 300, with a white line at your skill.

    Where a recipe is learned from an item, the first number is the skill that item asks for, and it is exact. What a trainer asks is decided on the server and no client carries it, so those levels are marked: † is what vanilla's trainers asked, for a recipe Forever has left where it was; ≈ is vanilla's level moved by as much as Forever moved the recipe's yellow level; ? is a recipe no item teaches and no vanilla trainer taught, so its level is not in the data.

    How the chance is estimated

    Two things are certain: an orange recipe gives a point every time, and a grey one never does. Blizzard has not published what happens in between. This page uses the straight line players have long used for Classic: certain up to the yellow level, then falling evenly to nothing at grey,

    chance = (grey − skill) ÷ (grey − yellow)

    which is 100% on the first yellow point, 50% as the recipe turns green and 0% as it turns grey, and averages 75% across the yellow band and 25% across the green one. Forever may not use exactly this curve, so read every percentage here as an estimate, good for comparing recipes and planning materials rather than for counting on the last craft.

    Working Overtime

    The tooltip does not say how that bonus is applied, and the arithmetic is on the server. So the page offers both readings: × multiplies the chance (at five ranks, 50% becomes 60%), + adds to it (50% becomes 70%). Either way an orange recipe stays certain and a grey one still gives nothing. × is the default because it is the lower of the two estimates.
