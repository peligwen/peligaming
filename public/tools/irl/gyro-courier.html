🚁 Gyro Courier

A single-seat open-frame gyrocopter — the engine behind your seat pushing, a free-wheeling two-blade rotor overhead doing the lifting — over Chattanooga, Tennessee, around Lovell Field (KCHA). The streets are empty: that is where you land.

How a gyro flies

The rotor is not driven in flight. It spins because air flows up through it, so the disc must always be tilted a little back into the airflow, and it must be kept loaded: a push-over that unloads it slows it, and a slow rotor cannot lift you. Watch the rotor gauge. The prerotator only spins it up on the ground; the takeoff roll brings it to flight speed.

Flying it

  1. ENGINE to start. Hold BRAKE, bring the throttle to about 40%, and hold PREROT until the rotor passes 200 rpm.
  2. Line up, full throttle, stick well back. As the rotor passes 300 rpm ease the stick forward; it lifts off around 40–45 kt. Climb at 45–50 kt.
  3. Cruise at 50–60 kt. The stick tilts the rotor head: back to slow and climb, forward to speed up. Throttle sets climb and descent. Rudder (the left stick, sideways) keeps the ball centred and steers on the ground.
  4. To land: approach at 40 kt, throttle low, flare over the street to walking pace, touch down, hold the stick back, brake. A gyro can land in a few metres; it can even descend vertically with no airspeed (about 2,000 ft/min, rotor spinning).
  5. Engine failure is not an emergency: glide at 45–50 kt and flare. Never push the nose over hard, and never let the rotor get slow.
