    Compass

    Alignment nudge: 0°

    Phone compasses drift; if the sky sits a few degrees off, drag it sideways while pointing to nudge the alignment, or centre a bright star you can see. On an iPhone the compass only reads true while the phone is tilted toward flat, so tilt it down for a moment now and then and the heading re-calibrates; upright, it holds the last good fix.

    View

    The pictures sit faintly behind the stars. The constellation in the middle of the screen is drawn brighter and named in the readout up top, and with the boundaries showing it is outlined; tap empty sky to see which constellation it belongs to.

    About

    Stars to magnitude 6 with their colours, constellation figures and the Messier objects from d3-celestial (Hipparcos data, BSD-3); the constellation pictures from Stellarium's modern sky culture (Free Art License), pinned to the sky by three stars each; the IAU boundaries as Delporte drew them on the 1875 equator, carried to today's; the Sun, Moon and planets from low-precision elements (about an arcminute); satellites from CelesTrak; magnetic declination from NOAA's WMM2025. Positions include precession to today's equinox but not refraction, so objects right on the horizon sit a little higher than drawn.

    📱 Point your phone at the sky

    Sky Pointer draws the sky for where you are and turns the phone into a window on it. It needs your location and the motion sensors — the browser will ask for both.
